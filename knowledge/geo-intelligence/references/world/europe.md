Scope: every European state plus TR, GE, AM, AZ, CY, IS, FO, GL (Danish realm), GI and the microstates; Russia with notes on its Asian part. Each country section opens with quick-fact bullets; full entries follow only for distinctive tells.
Plate formats, scripts, driving side and phone/postal formats are fixed by law (durable). Sign colours, bollards, poles and paint are conventions: old and new styles coexist for years, so every "since YEAR" is also a dating clue.
Entry format and rules: [index.md](index.md). Street View coverage per country, including the 2025–2026 launches in Bosnia and Herzegovina, Cyprus, Georgia and Kosovo: [streetview.md](streetview.md) §3. Coverage changes yearly; `pano.py coverage <lat,lon> --radius 400` is the live check.

## Region-wide patterns

### Driving side, units, plate families
- Left-hand traffic: GB, IE, MT, CY (whole island), Isle of Man, Jersey, Guernsey. Everyone else drives right, Gibraltar included. mph in the UK and the Crown Dependencies; Ireland is metric since 20 Jan 2005.
- EU band (12 stars + code, left edge): EU members; older plates without it persist. Blue band without stars: TR, RS, ME, BA, MK, XK (AL has a non-EU band too). National flag + code: UA, MD, GE, AM, AZ, BY, NO, IS, FO (left); RU puts flag + RUS in the right-hand region box. No band: CH, LI, GB (optional UK identifier since 28 Sep 2021, GB before); arms instead of a band: MC, AD, SM.
- Colour outliers: yellow front and rear NL, LU; yellow rear only GB, GI, older CY; dark-red characters BE; red border DK (and GL); black LI; blue characters MC, SM, IS, FO; red-white-red edge stripes AT; green EV plates HU.
- Restricted letter sets: RU, UA, BG use only Cyrillic letters that look Latin (RU, BG: А В Е К М Н О Р С Т У Х; UA: І instead of У); GR uses 14 Greek letters that look Latin; BA uses A E J K M O T only.
- Plates that encode a region: DE, AT, CH, PL, CZ, RO, BG, HR, RS, ME, SI, MK, XK, GR, TR, RU, UA, BY, AM, AZ, IE (county), GB (memory tag), SK (to 2022), FR (owner-chosen), IT (optional, rare). Not regional: NL, BE, LU, ES (since 2000), PT, DK, NO, SE, FI, EE, LV, LT, HU, AL (since 2011), GE, BA, IS. MD: unverified.

### Scripts and letters (one letter can close a case; confirm with `textgeo.py --text "<string>"`)
- Latin: þ → IS; ð → IS, FO; ø æ → NO, DK, FO (æ also IS); å → SE, NO, DK, Swedish in FI; õ (without ã) → EE; ő ű → HU; ł → PL; ą ę → PL, LT; ė į ų → LT; ā ē ī ū ķ ļ ņ ģ → LV; ř ů ě → CZ; ľ ĺ ŕ ô → SK; ș ț ă â î → RO, MD; ć đ → HR, BA, RS, ME; ś ź → PL, ME; frequent ë with ç → AL, XK; ı İ ş ğ → TR, AZ; ə → AZ; ċ ġ ħ għ → MT (ż also PL); ñ → ES; ã õ ç → PT; ß → DE, AT (never CH); l·l → Catalan; ŵ ŷ → Welsh; grave accents (à ò) → Scottish Gaelic vs acute (á ó) → Irish.
- Cyrillic: ї є ґ → UA (і also BY); ў → BY; ы э → RU or BY; ъ as a frequent vowel → BG (also rare in RU); ђ ћ → RS, BA, ME (џ also MK); ѓ ќ ѕ → MK; щ absent in BY, RS, MK. Street abbreviations: "ул." RU, BG; "вул." UA, BY.
- Greek → GR, CY. Georgian Mkhedruli → GE. Armenian → AM. Bilingual minority signs narrow regions: see each country.

### Yellow-background warning triangles
- Look for: a permanent red-bordered warning triangle with a yellow/amber field
- Points to: SE, FI, IS, PL, GR (national standards); legacy yellow from the Yugoslav standard survives in parts of SI, HR, BA, RS, ME, MK, XK. Ireland uses yellow DIAMONDS instead. White field elsewhere, notably NO, DK, DE, AT, CZ, SK, RU, TR, GB
- Strength: strong for that country set when the sign is clearly permanent; medium otherwise
- Counterexamples: roadworks triangles are yellow in many white-field countries (DE, FR, IT, ES, NL, NO); faded white signs look cream at low sun; former-Yugoslav states mix both
- Verify: `refsheet.py countries SE,NO,PL,CZ --n 6`; then plates and language
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warning_sign, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_signs_in_Greece, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_signs_in_Norway, all checked 2026-10

### Yellow centre line
- Look for: a yellow line between opposing lanes on a two-way road, white edge lines
- Points to: NO (standard; NO also paints the left edge of divided roads yellow); FI in imagery before about 2020–2023 (yellow barrier and centre lines; white for all permanent lines under the Road Traffic Act in force June 2020, 3-year transition); occasionally RU (allowed, first on a Murmansk Oblast highway)
- Strength: strong for NO with white warning triangles and Nordic scenery; medium for FI (date-dependent); weak for RU
- Counterexamples: temporary yellow at roadworks in DE, FR, IT, NL, ES, IS; Croatian yellow bus-lane lines; yellow edge lines (IE) and yellow kerb lines (GB, MT, CY) are not centre lines; Austria used yellow lane lines until 1995
- Verify: `refsheet.py countries NO,SE,FI --n 6`; date the image before scoring FI
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_surface_marking checked 2026-10; autotalli.com article on the 2020 Finnish marking reform checked 2026-10

### Direction, town and route-number signs
- Motorway signs green: IT, CH, TR, FI, HR, SI, RS; SE per its encyclopaedia page (one source disagrees). Blue: DE, AT, FR, ES, PT, GB, IE, NL, LU, PL, NO; BE blue on the motorway, green on approach. Others: (unverified)
- Ordinary roads: DE yellow (Bundesstraße); FR and GB green primary / white local; IE green national / white regional; PL green; IT blue extra-urban / white urban; CH blue main / white secondary; NL blue on every class; SE blue; TR blue state roads, white local
- Town-entry signs: DE yellow with black text; CH blue on main roads, white on secondary; NL blue; FR white with red border
- Route shields: DE A white-on-blue, B black-on-yellow; FR A and N white-on-red, D black-on-yellow; PL national white-on-red, voivodeship black-on-yellow; ES A/AP white-on-blue, N white-on-red, regional networks orange/green/yellow; E-routes green with white text (not signed in GB/IE)
- STOP reads "STOP" almost everywhere; "DUR" in TR and northern Cyprus. No yellow priority-road diamonds in GB or IE

### Bollard (delineator post) reflectors
- Look for: the reflector on posts on your side of the road (right side in right-driving countries): colour, shape, band
- Points to: white rectangle (right) + two white dots (left) on a black band: DE (same layout, colours unverified: NO, SE, FI, EE, LV, PT, LU, LI); red right / white left: AT, IT, NL, PL, SK, HU, SI, HR, GR, RO, BG, TR, RU, BY; yellow-amber right: ES, BE, DK, LT; orange right (often two): CZ; FR: white posts with a black oblique band, same reflector both sides
- Strength: medium for DE and PL (national specs); weak elsewhere (one encyclopaedic source, sometimes contradicted)
- Counterexamples: sources conflict for CZ, SK, IT and UA; designs vary by road authority and age; posts are rare in RO, BG and GB
- Verify: `refsheet.py countries CZ,SK,PL,AT --n 6` and compare posts side by side
- Source: de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leitpfosten checked 2026-10; gov.pl/web/gddkia-wroclaw (słupki prowadzące) checked 2026-10

### Crossings, chevrons, markers, guardrails
- Zebras: CH and LI yellow; RU alternating yellow-white (common since about 2014, some cities reverting since about 2020); GB and IE Belisha beacons (black-white poles, amber globes) with zigzag lines; mostly white elsewhere
- Curve chevrons: FR blue-white (J4); DE red-white; GB black-white; IE black on yellow (FR checked; DE, GB, IE general knowledge, medium); IT red-white (unverified). FR also: J3 white posts with a red band at junctions
- Markers: FR red-capped (N) / yellow-capped (D) kilometre stones (older roads); NL and BE hectometre posts every 100 m. Guardrail styles: (unverified); compare with `refsheet.py countries`

### Buildings, roofs, poles, vegetation (all weak alone)
- Roofs: low-pitch Roman tiles → Mediterranean (PT, ES, southern FR, IT, Adriatic, GR, TR, CY); steep red plain tiles → DE, AT, CH, CZ, SK, PL, HU; slate → Wales, Brittany, inland Galicia, Ardennes, Mosel; coloured corrugated metal → IS, Nordic, ex-Soviet, GE, AM; corrugated asbestos-cement → ex-Soviet, PL, Balkans; turf → FO, IS heritage, NO cabins; thatch → England, IE, NL, DK, northern DE; flat roofs with water tanks and solar heaters → GR, CY, MT, TR, coastal AL
- Concrete flat-roof houses with rebar left for a future floor: GR, AL, XK, MK, ME, TR, CY. Soviet panel blocks: former Eastern Bloc. Yellow above-ground gas pipes: GE, AM, AZ, RU, UA
- Poles: wooden with crossarms in GB, IE, Nordics, Baltics, rural PL and ex-Soviet countryside (often bolted to a concrete stub); concrete in the south and centre; perforated concrete poles in HU (single source); few overhead wires in NL, DK, BE and German towns (buried distribution)
- Vegetation: olive → Mediterranean; cork oak → Alentejo, Extremadura, Sardinia; eucalyptus plantations → PT, Galicia, Asturias; stone pine → Iberia, IT, TR coasts; poplar rows → Po valley, FR, HU; tea → Rize (TR), Guria/Adjara (GE); hazelnut → TR Black Sea, western GE; treeless → IS, FO, Arctic NO, Scottish islands

## United Kingdom (GB)
- Plates: black on white front, black on yellow rear; GB `AB12 CDE` since Sep 2001 = memory tag (first letter = region) + age identifier (March = year, e.g. 24; September = year + 50, e.g. 74) + 3 random letters. Northern Ireland: `ABZ 1234`-style, letter groups always contain I or Z. Crown Dependencies have own plates (codes GBM, GBJ, GBG, GBA)
- Paint: white centre and edge lines; single/double yellow kerb lines; red lines on London red routes; painted SLOW, KEEP CLEAR
- Signs: Transport lettering; motorway blue, primary routes green with yellow route numbers, other roads white; white warning triangles; zebras with Belisha beacons
- Phone +44 (020 London, 0121 Birmingham, 0161 Manchester, 0131 Edinburgh, 0141 Glasgow, 029 Cardiff, 028 Northern Ireland; 07 mobile). Postcodes `SW1A 1AA` (EH Edinburgh, G Glasgow, CF Cardiff, BT Northern Ireland). TLD .uk (.scot, .wales, .cymru, .london)

### Yellow rear plate + left-hand traffic
- Look for: white front plates, yellow rear plates, traffic keeping left
- Points to: GB (incl. Northern Ireland); also CY on older plates
- Strength: strong with mph signs
- Counterexamples: GI has the same plates but drives right; NL and LU plates are yellow at both ends; British cars abroad (IE, Spanish coasts, Channel ports)
- Verify: `clues.py lookup driving-side GB`; `refsheet.py countries GB,IE,CY --n 6`
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_the_United_Kingdom checked 2026-10

### Regions inside the United Kingdom
- Memory tag first letter: A East Anglia, B Birmingham, C Wales (Cymru), D Deeside to Shrewsbury, E Essex, F Forest and Fens, G Kent, H Hampshire and Dorset, K Borehamwood/Luton, L London, M Manchester and Merseyside, N North, O Oxford, P Preston, R Reading, S Scotland, V Severn Valley, W West of England, Y Yorkshire. Medium: dealers and leased fleets register far from home
- Wales: bilingual signs everywhere (Welsh first in much of the north and west), ARAF (= slow) painted on roads, "Llan-", "Aber-", "Pen-". Scotland: Gaelic-English signs in the Highlands and Islands, Gaelic on railway stations nationwide. Northern Ireland: NI plates, BT postcodes, 028 numbers, kerbs painted in flag colours in some areas
- England: building stone tracks geology (Cotswold limestone, northern gritstone, East Anglian flint and thatch, Cornish granite and "Tre-/Pol-/Pen-" names); hedgerows in lowlands, dry-stone walls in uplands. Verify: `refsheet.py regions GB:Wales,GB:Scotland`

## Ireland (IE)
- Plates: black on white front AND rear, EU band IRL, county name in Irish above the number; `YYS-CC-NNNNN`: year (3 digits since 2013: 131 = Jan–Jun, 132 = Jul–Dec), county code, serial
- Paint: white centre lines, YELLOW edge and hard-shoulder lines. Signs: yellow diamond warning signs; speed limits in km/h with "km/h" printed; green national-road signs, white regional/local, blue motorway; Irish name in italics with the English name; Irish only in the Gaeltacht. Post boxes green
- Phone +353 (01 Dublin, 021 Cork, 091 Galway, 061 Limerick; 08x mobile). Eircode `A65 F4E2` (routing key D01–D24 Dublin, T12 Cork, H91 Galway, V94 Limerick). TLD .ie

### Irish road furniture set
- Look for: yellow diamond warning signs, yellow edge lines, all-white plates with a year-county-serial pattern, left-hand traffic
- Points to: IE (not Northern Ireland, which has GB-style triangles, white edges, yellow rear plates and mph)
- Strength: strong (two or more of the set)
- Counterexamples: border roads where IE and NI furniture meet; pre-2005 imagery may still show mph
- Verify: `refsheet.py countries IE,GB --n 6`
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_European_road_signs and en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_surface_marking checked 2026-10

### Regions inside Ireland
- County code on plates (registration county for life): D Dublin, C Cork, G Galway, L Limerick, W Waterford, T Tipperary, KY Kerry, DL Donegal, MO Mayo, WX Wexford, KE Kildare, MH Meath, LH Louth, SO Sligo. Gaeltacht (Irish-only signs) → west Galway, west Donegal, west Kerry, parts of Mayo. West: stone walls, bog, few trees; east and south: hedged pasture. Verify: `refsheet.py regions "IE:Connacht,IE:Munster"` (Irish regions are the four provinces)

## France (FR)
- Plates: SIV `AB-123-CD` since 2009, white front and rear, EU band F, right blue band with a department number + regional logo (owner's choice). Older FNI `123 ABC 75` (department at the end), yellow rear on old cars
- Paint: white. Signs: town entry white with red border; green primary, white local, blue motorway; route shields A/N red, D yellow, E green; J4 curve chevrons blue-white; J6 delineators white with black oblique band; J3 white posts with red band at junctions. Post boxes yellow
- Phone +33, 10 digits: 01 Île-de-France, 02 north-west, 03 north-east, 04 south-east + Corsica, 05 south-west; 06/07 mobile. Postcodes 5 digits, first two = department (Corsica 20). TLD .fr (.paris, .bzh, .alsace, .corsica)

### Department number in the right-hand plate band
- Look for: a blue band on the RIGHT end of the plate with a 2–3 digit number under a regional logo
- Points to: FR; the number is the department the owner chose, usually the home one
- Strength: strong for FR; weak for the department since 2009 (free choice); FNI plates (department at the end) were tied to the owner's department

## Commonly confused

Run `refsheet.py countries <A,B> --n 6` for any pair, then apply the decisive test.
- **ES / PT**: plate (ES digits first `1234 BCD` vs PT pairs `AA-00-AA`, yellow strip 1998–2020); "calle/Ayuntamiento" + ñ vs "rua/Câmara" + ã õ "-ção"; postcode 5 digits vs `1234-567`. Galicia vs Minho look identical (granite, eucalyptus, hórreos): Galician "rúa/concello" with Spanish plates
- **CZ / SK**: ř ů ě vs ä ô ľ ĺ ŕ; CZ plate `1A2 3456` vs SK district code or `AA 123 AA` with state arms; "náměstí" vs "námestie"; Hungarian bilingual signs → SK
- **PL / CZ / SK**: yellow warning triangles → PL; ł and "ul." → PL; PL green direction signs and red/yellow route plates; PL voivodeship-letter plates
- **NO / SE / FI**: yellow centre + WHITE triangle → NO; white lines + yellow triangle → SE (or new FI); yellow line + yellow triangle → old FI. Plates: NO `AB 12345` with flag, SE `ABC 123`, FI `ABC-123`. Language: æ ø vs ä ö vs Finnish double vowels
- **DK / DE / NL**: plates (DK red border, DE district code and seals, NL yellow); DE yellow town signs and black-band posts; NL blue signs, red cycle paths; DK æ ø, "vej"
