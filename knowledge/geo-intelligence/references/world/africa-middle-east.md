## Region-wide patterns

### Traffic side
- Left-hand traffic: ZA, LS, SZ, BW, NA, ZW, ZM, MW, MZ, TZ, KE, UG, MU and Seychelles. All other African countries and every Middle East country drive on the right.
- Right-hand exceptions near the left-hand bloc: Rwanda and Burundi (Belgian heritage), Ethiopia (since 1964), Somalia, Sudan (1973). Former British West Africa switched to the right: Gambia 1965, Sierra Leone 1971, Nigeria 1972, Ghana 1974; South Yemen 1977.
- Wheel side is not traffic side: right-hand-drive imports run in some right-hand countries (common in south-eastern DR Congo). Read lane position and parked cars. Verify: `clues.py lookup driving-side left`.

### Languages and scripts
- French: Maghreb (second sign language), francophone West and Central Africa, Madagascar, Burundi, Rwanda (with English and Kinyarwanda), Lebanon, Mauritius (beside English), Réunion. English: Commonwealth Africa; second sign language in Israel, Jordan, Egypt and the Gulf. Portuguese: Mozambique, Angola, Guinea-Bissau, Cabo Verde, São Tomé.
- Tifinagh (ⵜⴰⵎⴰⵣⵉⵖⵜ): official Amazigh orthography in Morocco (adopted 2003; Amazigh official since 2011). Algeria made Tamazight official in 2016, but it is written mostly in Latin letters and appears mostly in Kabylie. Traditional Tuareg Tifinagh is used in the Sahara (southern Algeria, Mali, Niger).
- Ge'ez/Ethiopic syllabary (ሀ ለ መ ሰ ቤ): Ethiopia (Amharic, Tigrinya) and Eritrea (Tigrinya). Hebrew: Israel only.
- Latin-script African languages with telltale letters: Wolof ë ñ (Senegal); Bambara/Dioula ɛ ɔ ɲ ŋ (Mali, Burkina, Côte d'Ivoire); Akan/Twi ɛ ɔ and Ewe ɖ ƒ ŋ ʋ (Ghana, Togo); Hausa ɓ ɗ ƙ ƴ (northern Nigeria, Niger); Yoruba ẹ ọ ṣ with tone marks (south-west Nigeria, Benin); Igbo ị ọ ụ ṅ (south-east Nigeria); Somali x, c, dh and doubled vowels; Oromo doubled vowels with dh, x, q; Malagasy lacks c, q, u, w, x and ends many words in -y; isiZulu/isiXhosa click letters c, q, x and prefixes isi-, um-, kwa-; Afrikaans -straat, -weg, -fontein, -dorp. Verify: `textgeo.py --text "<string>"`.

### Arabic script and numerals
- The Maghreb (MA, DZ, TN; Libyan plates) writes Western digits 0–9 even inside Arabic text. Egypt, Sudan, the Levant, Iraq and the Gulf use Eastern Arabic digits ٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩, often beside Western ones.
- Persian adds پ چ ژ گ, writes ک and a final ی without dots, and uses its own digit shapes ۴ ۵ ۶ (Arabic ٤ ٥ ٦). Sorani Kurdish adds ڕ ڵ ێ ۆ ە; Pashto adds ټ ډ ړ ښ ږ ځ څ; Urdu adds ٹ ڈ ڑ ں ے.
- Counterexamples: Gulf and Iraqi dialect spellings use چ and گ; Egyptian print often drops the dots of a final ي.

### Plate colour systems
- White front + yellow rear: Kenya, Rwanda, Burundi, Botswana, Algeria (digits only); Uganda's old series and Tanzania probably (sources conflict).
- Yellow front and rear: Israel, Namibia, Oman (private), Zimbabwe private plates (white also issued since 2006). Older series: Eswatini (1980–2011), Tanzania (1980s).
- Black background, white characters: Tunisia, Madagascar; Mozambique before 2011.
- Other: Cameroon orange; Mozambique white-to-blue gradient; Palestine green characters; Nigeria, Lesotho, Gauteng and new KwaZulu-Natal plates use blue characters on white.
- Vehicle class shown by a coloured strip: Egypt (top), Saudi Arabia (side strip), Jordan (green for taxis and buses); by the whole plate in Lebanon (red = taxis, public transport).
- Regional code on the plate: ZA, NA, MZ, MG, SN, GH, NG, CM, CI (pre-2023), ET (pre-2026), MA, DZ, EG, LY, PS, LB, IQ, IR, YE, SZ, AE (emirate design). None: KE, UG, TZ, RW, BW, IL, JO, SA, QA, KW, BH, TN.

### Yellow left edge line with white centre line (SADC manual)
- Look for: solid yellow line along the outer edge of the carriageway, white broken or solid centre line; on dual carriageways a white line on the median side
- Points to: the SADC states using the regional manual (ZA, BW, NA, LS, SZ, ZW, ZM, MW, MZ, TZ); with left-hand traffic it nearly fixes Southern Africa
- Strength: medium (set by the manual: left edge RM4.1 yellow, right edge RM4.2 white, no-overtaking line RM1 white; rural roads often have no paint)
- Counterexamples: Israel also uses yellow shoulder lines (right-hand traffic, Hebrew); divided highways in many countries put yellow on the median side; temporary road-works lines are yellow almost everywhere; Kenya's edge lines are white (single source)
- Verify: `refsheet.py countries ZA,BW,NA,KE --n 6`
- Source: SADC Road Traffic Signs Manual vol. 1 ch. 7 (sartsma.co.za, May 2012 edition) checked 2026-10; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_surface_marking (Israel) checked 2026-10

Other paint: Kenya white edges with solid yellow no-overtaking centre lines (kenyans.co.ke, single source); Nigeria yellow centre lines between opposing directions (single source); French-influenced North and West Africa white lines (general knowledge, weak). Gulf states, Iran and the Levant: not verified.

### Soil, vegetation, terrain (weak without a month)
- Mediterranean strip (MA–TN coast, Levant): olives, Aleppo pine, eucalyptus windbreaks, prickly pear; green in winter, brown in summer.
- Sahara and Arabian deserts: hamada, dunes, date-palm oases. Sahel (SN, ML, BF, NE, northern NG): scattered acacia and baobab on sand. Toward the Gulf of Guinea: savanna, then oil palms and rainforest; red laterite roads and walls.
- East African highlands (KE, UG, RW, BI, ET): red soil, eucalyptus, banana, tea, volcanic cones; dry acacia-commiphora bush in the lowlands.
- Miombo woodland (ZM, MW, MZ, ZW, southern TZ); mopane (northern BW, southern ZW, Limpopo, northern NA); Kalahari flat sand and thorn bush (BW, eastern NA, Northern Cape); Namib dunes and gravel plains.
- Madagascar highlands: grass with red erosion gullies (lavaka) and rice terraces. Iran: wet Caspian strip versus dry plateau. Asir (SA) and Dhofar (OM): green, foggy mountains in the summer monsoon.

### Buildings
- Flat-roofed concrete with rebar left for another floor: Egypt, the Levant, Iraq, the Gulf, the Maghreb and much of West Africa.
- Sudano-Sahelian mud brick: Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, northern Nigeria. Round huts (rondavels): Lesotho, Eswatini, Eastern Cape, KwaZulu-Natal, rural Botswana.
- Cape Dutch gables: Western Cape. German colonial facades: Swakopmund, Lüderitz. Corrugated-iron roofs: East and Central Africa. Two-storey brick houses with verandas: Malagasy highlands.
- Jerusalem stone and rooftop solar heaters: Israel. Adobe and windcatchers: central Iran. Mud-brick tower houses: Yemen.
- Poles: no country-level pole convention was verified for this region; compare with `refsheet.py countries <ISO2,ISO2> --n 6` instead of memory.

## Morocco (MA)

**Quick facts**
- Right-hand. Plates white front and rear: serial (up to 5 digits), an Arabic letter (أ ب د ه و ط ي) between two bars, then a prefecture/province number 1–89. Temporary `123456 WW`; dealer `12345 W 18` red on white.
- Signs follow French designs, in Arabic and French; Tamazight in Tifinagh on motorways (since 2015, sometimes replacing French) and on public buildings. Text on white signs changed from dark blue to black in 2019.
- Phone +212; landlines 05: 052x Casablanca–Settat, Marrakech, Safi, Agadir and the south (05289 Laâyoune, Dakhla); 053x Rabat, Kénitra, Fès, Meknès, Oujda, Tangier, Tétouan. Mobiles 06, 07. Postal 5 digits. TLD .ma.
- No official Google Street View. Street level comes from elsewhere: search for local and historical panorama sites (one example is carte.ma, 2013–2015 panoramas of several cities), Google user photo spheres and place photos (browser), Mapillary and KartaView (patchy). List the region's towns (`geodata.py towns MA --admin1 …`) and compare their fabric on satellite. Source: web search 2026-10.

### Moroccan plate: Arabic letter between bars + region number
- Look for: one-line white plate `serial ‖ letter ‖ number`; two-line plates put the letter and number on top
- Points to: Morocco; the number is the issuing prefecture: 1 Rabat, 6–13 and 72–74 Casablanca, 14 Mohammedia, 15–16 Fès, 20–21 Meknès, 26–28 Marrakech, 33–34 Agadir, 40–41 Tangier, 44 Tétouan, 48 Oujda, 50 Nador, 59 Kénitra; 63, 68–71 and 89 are the southern provinces (Laâyoune–Sakia El Hamra and Dakhla–Oued Ed-Dahab regions)
- Strength: strong for the country (national format); medium for the region when several local cars agree
- Counterexamples: cars travel, and company fleets register at head office; diplomatic (blue), government (white on black) and trailer (red) plates follow other formats
- Verify: read the number against en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Morocco; web search images of the prefecture
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Morocco checked 2026-10

### Tifinagh on official signs
- Look for: Neo-Tifinagh (circles, crosses, chevrons, e.g. ⵜⴰⵎⴰⵣⵉⵖⵜ) on public-building plaques, motorway and municipal signs, with Arabic and often French
- Points to: Morocco (state policy). In Algeria it is rarer and concentrated in Kabylie (Tizi Ouzou, Béjaïa, Bouira), where Latin-script Kabyle is more common
- Strength: strong on official signage (MA against DZ/TN); medium on shop signs or graffiti
- Counterexamples: Amazigh flags and Tifinagh slogans appear at cultural sites across the Maghreb and in diaspora areas; Libya's Amazigh towns use it informally (unverified)
- Verify: `textgeo.py --text "<transcribed Tifinagh>"`; check which other scripts share the sign
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_signs_in_Morocco and /wiki/Tifinagh checked 2026-10; Algerian signage studies via web search 2026-10

### Petit-taxi colour = city
- Look for: small city taxis painted one colour, with the city name and number on a roof sign
- Points to: Casablanca red, Rabat dark blue, Salé yellow, Tangier light blue (often a yellow stripe), Agadir orange, Marrakech beige/ochre, Fès red
- Strength: medium (municipal liveries; sources agree on the main cities)
- Counterexamples: several cities share red (Casablanca, Fès, Oujda, Nador) or blue; Marrakech is described as beige, ochre, gold or yellow; liveries get refreshed
- Verify: read the roof sign; web search "petit taxi <city> couleur"
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_of_Morocco, moroccoworldnews.com and travel guides via web search 2026-10

**Regions inside Morocco**
- North (Tangier–Tétouan, Rif): steep green hills, Mediterranean coast, whitewashed or blue-washed towns (Chefchaouen); plates 40–47, 75; 053x phones.
- Atlantic plain (Kénitra–Rabat–Casablanca–El Jadida): flat farmland, eucalyptus windbreaks, dense suburbs; plates 1–4, 6–14, 55, 58–59, 72–74, 81.
- Middle Atlas (Ifrane, Azrou): cedar and oak forest, steep roofs for snow. High Atlas and the south (Marrakech, Ouarzazate, Drâa, Tinghir): ochre earth-coloured houses and kasbahs, palm groves; Marrakech facades are uniformly ochre-pink (general knowledge).
- Souss (Agadir, Taroudant): argan trees, greenhouses; plates 33–37. Oriental (Oujda, Nador, Figuig): steppe and bare hills; plates 48–53.
- Southern provinces (Laâyoune–Sakia El Hamra, Dakhla–Oued Ed-Dahab: Laâyoune, Boujdour, Smara, Dakhla): flat hamada, Atlantic cliffs, few settlements; Moroccan plates (63, 68–71, 89), phones (05289) and signs. `geodata.py towns MA --admin1 "Laayoune-Sakia El Hamra"` lists their towns.

## Algeria (DZ)

**Quick facts**
- Right-hand. Plates are digits only, black on white at the front and on yellow at the rear: `02639 126 09` = serial, class digit (1 = private) + year of manufacture (26), wilaya code (09 Blida). Wilayas 49–58 got codes in 2022; 59–69 from January 2027.
- Signs in French design, Arabic + French; Tamazight (mostly Latin script) in Kabylie.
- Phone +213; landlines 021/023 Algiers, 041 Oran, 026 Tizi Ouzou, 036 Sétif, 038 Annaba; mobiles 05 (Ooredoo), 06 (Mobilis), 07 (Djezzy). Postal 5 digits, first two = wilaya. TLD .dz. No official Street View.

### Algerian all-digit plate, yellow at the rear
- Look for: three groups of digits, no letters; yellow plate on the rear
- Points to: Algeria; last two digits = wilaya (16 Algiers, 31 Oran, 25 Constantine, 15 Tizi Ouzou, 06 Béjaïa, 19 Sétif, 23 Annaba, 13 Tlemcen, 30 Ouargla); the middle group dates the car
- Strength: strong (national format, colours fixed by law)
- Counterexamples: a front view loses the yellow cue; Kenyan and Botswanan rear plates are also yellow but contain letters
- Verify: wilaya table in en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Algeria
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Algeria checked 2026-10

## Tunisia (TN)

**Quick facts**
- Right-hand. Plates white on black: `123 تونس 4567`; the left number is a series that rises over time (about 131–140 in 2008–09, 194–208 in 2017–18); rental cars white on blue; government red on white.
- Arabic + French signs, French designs. Olive groves of the Sahel; whitewashed houses with blue doors around Tunis.
- Phone +216, 8 digits, no trunk 0; landlines 7x (Tunis 70/71, Sousse 73); mobiles 2x, 4x, 5x, 9x. Postal 4 digits. TLD .tn. Official SV since 2017.

### Black plate with تونس in the middle
- Look for: black plate, white digits, the word تونس between two numbers
- Points to: Tunisia; the series number gives an approximate registration year
- Strength: strong
- Counterexamples: Madagascar's plates are also white on black but carry Latin letters; Mozambique (pre-2011) and old Kenyan plates were black; Tunisian rental plates are blue
- Verify: `refsheet.py countries TN,DZ,MA --n 6`
- Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Tunisia checked 2026-10

## Libya (LY)
- Right-hand. Plates since 2013 black on white: municipality code (1–2 digits), dash, serial, with ليبيا at the right; agricultural and commercial plates black on yellow.
- Arabic signage; +218; no postal codes; TLD .ly; no Street View. Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Libya checked 2026-10.

## Egypt (EG)

**Quick facts**
- Right-hand. Plates since 2008 (35×17 cm): top strip "EGYPT مصر" coloured by class: light blue private, orange taxi, red truck, grey bus/government, beige tourist, yellow customs, green diplomatic, dark blue police. Below: Arabic letters and Eastern Arabic digits only (Latin dropped).
- Digit and letter count hint at the governorate: 3 digits = Cairo, 2 letters = Giza, elsewhere 4 digits + 3 letters with a fixed governorate letter (Alexandria س, Qalyubia ق, Sharqia ر, Dakahlia د, Gharbia ع); Qena/Luxor/Aswan use ص pairs, Canal/Sinai/Red Sea/Damietta ط pairs, Matrouh/New Valley ج pairs (single source).
- Arabic + English signs. Phone +20; Cairo/Giza 02, Alexandria 03, Mansoura 050, Ismailia 064, Arish 068, Asyut 088, Luxor 095, Aswan 097; mobiles 010, 011, 012, 015. TLD .eg. Street View: landmarks only (Giza, Saqqara, Cairo).

### Egyptian plate: coloured top strip, Arabic-only characters
- Look for: near-square plate, coloured band reading EGYPT/مصر, then Arabic letters and Eastern digits with no Latin row
- Points to: Egypt; band colour = vehicle class; digit/letter pattern = governorate

## Commonly confused

- **KE / UG / TZ** (all left-hand, Swahili on shops): the plate's first letter decides (K…, U…, T…); Kenyan K-series and older Ugandan plates share the `LLL 123L` shape, Tanzanian puts the T alone first. Official signs: English in KE and UG, Swahili in TZ. Safaricom green: KE. Landline codes: Nairobi 020 versus Kampala 041 versus Dar es Salaam 022. Tanzania has only landmark Street View.
- **RW / UG / BI**: Rwanda and Burundi drive on the right, Uganda on the left. Rwandan `RAB 123C` versus Ugandan `UAA 123A`/`UA 123AB`. Kinyarwanda/English (RW) versus Kirundi/French (BI) versus English/Luganda (UG). Burundi has no Street View.
- **ZA / BW / NA / LS / SZ / ZW**: plates first: ZA province suffix or C-prefix; BW `B 123 ABC` white/yellow; NA all-yellow `N 1234 W`; LS blue on white `A 1234`; SZ `xSD 123 xx` on a landscape; ZW `ABC 1234`. Language: Afrikaans → ZA/NA; Sesotho with oe/li → LS; siSwati → SZ (also in Mpumalanga); Setswana → BW (also North West). Terrain: LS treeless mountains; BW flat sand; NA desert and gravel C/D roads. Route numbers: ZA N/R/M, BW A, NA B/C/D. Zimbabwe has no official Street View.
- **ZM / ZW / MW**: all left-hand and English, none with official Street View. Zambia and Zimbabwe both use `ABC 1234`; Malawi uses two letters + digits. Chichewa (MW), Bemba/Nyanja (ZM), Shona/Ndebele (ZW).
- **GH / NG / CI / TG / BJ**: English (GH, NG) versus French (CI, TG, BJ). Plates: GH region + year; NG US-size with state name; CI `AB-123-CD` (2023+) or older region digits; TG flag + TG at left; BJ map + RB at right. Only GH and NG have Street View.
- **SN / ML / BF**: Senegal plates carry an SN band and region code; Wolof ë/ñ; coastal, baobab country; only SN has car coverage. Mali: Bambara, Niger-river mud architecture. Burkina: `12 AB 1234` plates, Mooré, laterite plateau.
- **MA / DZ / TN**: plates decide: MA white with Arabic letter and region number; DZ digits only, yellow rear; TN white on black with تونس. Tifinagh on official signs → MA. Phones: +212 05/06/07, +213 0x + 8 digits, +216 8 digits. Only TN has Street View.
- **EG / JO**: Egyptian plate has a coloured top strip and only Arabic letters and Eastern digits; Jordanian plate uses Western digits with a JORDAN strip. Egypt: flat irrigated Nile land and dense red-brick blocks; Jordan: limestone hills and pale stone cubes. Jordan has car coverage; Egypt only landmarks.
- **IL / PS / JO / LB**: yellow plates → Israeli registration (also in settlements and East Jerusalem); green-on-white → Palestinian; white with JORDAN strip → Jordan; white with blue cedar band → Lebanon. Hebrew on road signs → Israel or an Israeli-run road in the West Bank. Mobiles: 05x IL, 059/056 PS, 077–079 JO, 03/7x LB. Lebanon: steep green mountains and red roofs; Jordan: arid; West Bank: terraced olive hills.
- **AE / QA / OM / SA / KW / BH**: emirate designs → UAE; digits-only with maroon section → Qatar; yellow → Oman; Arabic-over-Latin with KSA strip → Saudi; `12-34567` with "State of Kuwait" → Kuwait; digits-only with flag → Bahrain. Area codes 02–09 → UAE; 011–017 → Saudi; 8-digit numbers without area codes → QA/OM/KW/BH. Street View only in AE, QA, OM. Oman: mountains and whitewash; Qatar and Bahrain flat; Saudi interior plateau.
- **JO / KW**: both white `12-34567`-style plates; a JORDAN/الأردن strip versus "State of Kuwait" in Arabic. Jordan has Street View, Kuwait none.
- **IR / IQ / AF**: Persian letters and digits → Iran or Afghanistan; Pashto letters → Afghanistan; Arabic with Eastern digits → Iraq; Sorani letters ڕ ڵ ێ ۆ → Iraqi (or Iranian) Kurdistan. Plates: Iran Persian digits with a boxed province code; Iraq `GG X 1234` in Latin with an IRQ band (2022/2024+). Iranian-built cars dominate in Iran and also appear in Iraq and Afghanistan. Afghan details: [asia.md](asia.md#afghanistan-af).
- **MG / RE / MU**: Madagascar and Réunion drive on the right, Mauritius on the left. Plates: black Malagasy, French with 974, Mauritian month code. Malagasy words → MG; French chains and road furniture → RE; English signs with French shop names → MU.
- **Yellow plates (IL / NA / OM / ZW)**: Israel digits only with a blue IL band; Namibia N + digits + town suffix; Oman digits + one or two Latin letters with Arabic; Zimbabwe `ABC 1234` (white also issued). NA and ZW drive on the left, IL and OM on the right. Yellow at the rear only: KE, RW, BW, and DZ (digits only).
- **Black plates (TN / MG)**: Tunisia white on black with تونس between two numbers; Madagascar white on black with four digits and Latin province letters. Malagasy or Arabic text decides.
- **MZ / ST**: both Portuguese; Mozambique drives on the left with province-coded plates and has no official Street View; São Tomé and Príncipe drives on the right on small equatorial islands and has coverage.
- **SY / LB**: Syrian plates show the governorate name with Eastern digits, or Western digits with SYR (2025); Lebanese plates are white with a blue cedar band. Lebanon has car coverage; Syria none.
- **YE / OM**: Yemeni plates show a governorate number left of a dividing line and mud-brick tower houses; Omani private plates are yellow, houses whitewashed. Oman has car coverage; Yemen none.
- **ET / ER**: both Ge'ez script and right-hand. Eritrea adds Arabic and Italian-era architecture and has no Street View; Ethiopian plates show a region code (AA, OR, AM…) or the new ETH series.
